# Marketing and promotion

## 1. Pick a narrow pilot

Start with one actual neighbourhood and an adult teacher/guardian Study pilot. Choose based on real organiser availability and willingness to respond. India location presets do not establish nationwide coverage. Numeric recruitment targets are proposed goals, never achieved metrics.

## 2. Use accurate positioning

Nearby: **Discover nearby. Deal directly.** Explain free ordinary posting, 0% Paper Shapers transaction commission and no platform reservation fee beside venue-charge/tax/condition caveats. Study: chapter practice and formative feedback with teacher judgement retained. Do not promise grades, delivery, verified businesses, guaranteed bookings or universally lower prices.

## 3. Build supply first

Identify real local libraries, classes, cafes, services and community organisers. Send outreach only after the founder approves recipients/channel. Explain review, required content, private inbox and host response responsibilities. Help authorised organisers create accurate posts; never invent offers, imply partnership or secretly boost a listing.

## 4. Prepare approved copy

Merchant draft:

> Share a local event or offer on Paper Shapers Nearby. Ordinary posting is free and Paper Shapers takes no commission on direct transactions. Visitors can register interest or request one place when you enable reservations. You confirm availability in your local desk. Venue prices and offer conditions still apply.

Teacher draft:

> Try a short chapter-based practice paper, attempt it privately and use the formative review to choose what to revisit. Please check generated content against your syllabus and teaching judgement.

No messages, mailing lists, public campaigns or advertising accounts were created by this audit.

## 5. Use permission-based channels

After launch gates pass, use owned social profiles, original guides, approved venue QR posters and groups whose administrators permit promotion. Give one clear destination and invite useful feedback. Avoid scraped contacts, bulk DMs, unsolicited attendee marketing and unsupported school/competitor associations. Obtain permission for logos, photographs, testimonials and student work.

## 6. Stage the launch

Preparation: safety/support and actual flow acceptance. Pilot: recruit a small real organiser/teacher group and monitor responses manually. Next: fix confusion, content gaps and delays before buying traffic. Broader launch: publish substantiated stories and use actual acquisition/retention evidence. This is an execution sequence, not a promised timeline.

## 7. Measure the whole task

Nearby funnel: useful notice supply → discovery → interest → request → confirmation → cancellation/complaint. Study: planner → successful generation → attempt → useful review → repeat practice. Confirmation is not footfall. Track support cost, model attempts and failures alongside conversion, using aggregated reports rather than learner answers or attendee identities.

## 8. Decide whether to expand

Expand when hosts respond, safety is acceptable and the cost per useful action fits the actual budget. If supply is thin, improve onboarding before increasing traffic. Full consent-aware acquisition analytics, referral economics and attendance measurement are future work; never reward ad clicks or report invented growth.
